Unit 4: Probability Distribution

Discrete and Continuous Distributions

Introduction

In this lesson you will study Discrete and Continuous Distributions, part of Unit 4: Probability Distribution. Random variables and the distributions that describe them.

Learning Objectives

  • Explain Discrete and Continuous Distributions in your own words.
  • Use the key formula in a worked example.
  • Interpret the result in a real situation.

Explanation and Key Definitions

Discrete distributions, such as the binomial, give the probability of each count. Continuous distributions, such as the normal, use areas under a density curve.

Formula

$$P(X=k)=\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$$

Worked Example

Step-by-step solution
  1. Binomial with n = 3, p = 0.5 and k = 2
  2. C(3,2) = 3
  3. P = 3 × 0.5² × 0.5 = 0.375

Real-Life Applications

Modeling system failures, traffic arrivals and defect counts.
