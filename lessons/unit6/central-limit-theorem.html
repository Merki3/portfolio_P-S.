Unit 6: Sampling Distribution and Central Limit Theorem

Central Limit Theorem (CLT)

Introduction

In this lesson you will study Central Limit Theorem (CLT), part of Unit 6: Sampling Distribution and Central Limit Theorem. How sample means behave, and why the Central Limit Theorem matters.

Learning Objectives

  • Explain Central Limit Theorem (CLT) in your own words.
  • Use the key formula in a worked example.
  • Interpret the result in a real situation.

Explanation and Key Definitions

For large n (about 30 or more), the sampling distribution of x̄ is approximately normal, whatever the shape of the population.

Formula

$$\bar{x}\sim N\!\left(\mu,\ \frac{\sigma^2}{n}\right)$$

Worked Example

Step-by-step solution
  1. μ = 50, σ = 10, n = 25 so SE = 2
  2. P(x̄ > 52): z = (52 − 50)/2 = 1
  3. P(Z > 1) = 0.1587

Real-Life Applications

Polling and A/B testing depend on how much sample means vary.
